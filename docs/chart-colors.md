# Chart colors

Fourteen chart colors — ten categorical, four state — and the tone each takes
on each ground. Generated by `scripts/chart-palette.cjs` (read-only; run it
against any brand's bundled CSS with `DINO_LIB=/path node scripts/chart-palette.cjs`).

## The model

**Charts are drawn on exactly two grounds: `Surface-Brightest` and
`Surface-Dimmest`.** `--Chart-BG` names which. That is the whole reason this
stays tractable — the alternative reading, "whatever surface the chart happens
to sit on," would be ten levels × twelve themes.

**One tone per series per ground covers every theme.** Verified: a given
ground is close enough in lightness under all twelve themes that a single tone
clears 3:1 on all of them. So the table is **14 × 2 grounds × 2 modes = 56
values**, not 14 × 120.

**The `:root` rule needs no change.** Theme never enters: the ramps are
per-mode, not per-theme, so `Primary-Color-4` is one hex in light mode whatever
theme is active. Chart tokens sit at `:root` per mode, and the only selector
involved is `data-surface` — with two of its ten values.

**The `Charts: { Surfaces, Containers }` scaffold is the wrong shape.** It was
seeded in the Modes initializer (`exportColorSystem.ts:3566`, `:3586`) to mirror
`Text`/`Quiet`/`Border`/`Tag`, which genuinely need all ten surface levels and
both container branches. Charts need two entries. Collapse it rather than fill
it: a structure that claims a variation which does not exist is how the next
reader concludes charts are theme-scoped.

**Hue is identity; tone is legibility.** A series keeps one hue on every
ground — change it and the legend lies. Only the tone moves, to clear 3:1.

## Two requirements, and only one of them is WCAG

| | requirement | how it is met |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mark vs ground | 3:1, WCAG 1.4.11 | tone chosen per ground |
| series vs series | none in WCAG | a border, plus hue separation for the legend |

Adjacent segments in a stacked bar or pie are separated by a **border drawn in
`--Chart-BG`**. Understanding 1.4.11 waives adjacent-color contrast where a
visible boundary exists, and because every series already clears 3:1 against
the ground, a ground-colored border is visible by construction. The two
requirements collapse into one check.

What the border does *not* fix is the legend: matching a swatch in the key to
its neighbor is still a color-identification task. That is why separation is
measured as CIEDE2000 under **deuteranopia and protanopia** rather than as a
contrast ratio.

## Tones are solved jointly, not per series

An earlier version gave each series a "home" tone picked for separability and
let a per-series 3:1 search re-tone it on each ground. The search pushed
neighbors onto the same tone and ΔE collapsed to **0.3** — two series rendered
as one color, with every individual check passing. Lightness separation only
exists if the set is solved together, subject to 3:1, on each ground.

`TONE_BAND = [3, 10]` is a constraint, not a preference. Tones 1–2 and 11–12
are near-black and near-white and carry almost no chroma, so a series pushed
there reads as "black" rather than as itself. The optimizer will use them for
separation if allowed, and the series stops being recognizable across grounds.

## Results for this brand

Run the script for the current numbers. At the time of writing:

- **All fourteen clear 3:1** on every theme of both grounds, both modes.
- Worst legend ΔE per ground: 5.3 / 7.1 / **2.3** / 6.5.
- The weak ground is **Dark-Mode | Surface-Brightest**, where Primary and
  Secondary both compress toward near-black (ΔE 2.3).

Ten categorical rather than twelve costs almost nothing — worst legend ΔE 4.5
vs 4.7. The crowding is hue-space, not series count: with every series on one
tone, hue is the only separator and the arc is already full.

**Tone 6 is both the ramp's chroma peak (52) and the only tone clearing 3:1 on
BOTH grounds.** So the vivid colors are not a compromise — they are the most
saturated legal choice. The constraint on a light ground is chroma, not
nearness to the threshold: a contrast ceiling was tried and collapsed every
series onto one tone, killing legend separation (ΔE 2.7).

**Spreading tones helps on a dark ground and hurts on a light one.** On
Surface-Brightest the usable tones all sit on the dark side, so spreading moves
colors toward each other and strips chroma as lightness falls — ΔE 2.7 → 2.0,
with picks like `#2e2e2e`. `solveGround` therefore runs the hill-climb from two
seeds, max-chroma and spread, and keeps whichever measures better.

## Known limits

**The brand's own triad is not CVD-separable.** Primary, Secondary and
Tertiary sit at 23°, 64° and 115° — straddling the red–green axis that
protanopia and deuteranopia compress. No choice of six complements fixes that;
it is a property of the brand's seeds.

**Do not mix the categorical ten and the state four in one chart.** Most of the
remaining collisions are cross-set (Tertiary/Error, Primary/Success). Using one
set or the other removes them, and costs nothing.

**Ten CVD-distinguishable categorical colors is near the practical ceiling**
even for palettes designed with no other constraints. The borders are what make
ten workable.

## What Figma needs — 26 new variables

The brand four and the state four need **no new storage**: their tones are
already variables, so `Chart-Primary` on Light|Brightest is an alias to
`Primary-Color-4`, which carries its own Light/Dark values. Eight of the
fourteen cost nothing but the alias.

1. **Twelve literals in `Modes`** — the six new hues × two grounds, each with a
   Light-Mode and a Dark-Mode value. Created as
   `Chart/Surface-Brightest/Color-5…10` and `Chart/Surface-Dimmest/Color-5…10`. Literals live in `Modes` and everything
   else aliases into it by name, which is the rule the rest of the system
   already follows.

2. **The `Charts` collection gains two modes**: `Surface-Brightest` and
   `Surface-Dimmest`, replacing `Mode 1`. This is the ground axis; `Modes`
   supplies light/dark, so nothing holds a value twice.

3. **Fourteen color variables in `Charts`**, each an **alias** per mode — to the
   brand/state ramps for eight of them, to the new `Modes` literals for six.
   Aliases, never literals: a literal here would freeze one mode.

4. **`Chart-BG`** — alias per mode to that ground's `Background`.

5. **`Chart-Border-Width`** (FLOAT) — the only genuinely new token. The border
   *color* is `Chart-BG`, so no new color variable.

6. `Chart-Lines` already exists.

The studio should go on computing the full 12-tone ramp for each new hue
internally even though only four values are emitted: the optimizer picks from
the whole ramp, so if the brand's seeds move and tone 6 becomes right where
tone 5 was, that is a value change on an existing variable rather than a new
one. Which matters when the recovery story for Figma variables is "never
delete" (invariant 8).

**Chart marks have no hover or pressed state, decided 2026-10-07.** That is
what keeps this at 26 variables. If one is added later, the six new hues each
need a hover tone — twelve more literals, not a full ramp — because the brand
four and the state four can step through their ramps and the six cannot. Adding
it to only half the series is the failure mode to avoid.

Per invariant 5 the preview, the export and the Figma payload all change
together, with a parity test. Per invariant 8, nothing here deletes a Figma
variable — `Mode 1` is renamed, not removed.
